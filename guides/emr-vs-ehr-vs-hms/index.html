EMR vs EHR vs HMS vs PHR: what's the difference?

These four terms are often used as if they mean the same thing. In short: an EMR is one provider's clinical record, an EHR is a record designed to follow the patient between providers, an HMS runs the hospital's operations, and a PHR belongs to the patient.

EMR (electronic medical record)One clinic or hospitalDiagnoses, notes, prescriptions, results at that providerA doctor reviewing last month's visit

PHR (personal health record)The patientReports, prescriptions, readings and history the patient keepsA family keeping parents' reports in one place

How they fit together in India

ABDM (Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission) links these pieces: patients get an ABHA number, facilities register in the Health Facility Registry, and records can move between providers with the patient's consent. A hospital's HMS holds its EMR; the patient's PHR app can receive copies; consent decides who sees what.

Frequently asked questions

Is an HMS the same as an EMR?

No. An HMS runs hospital operations (appointments, beds, pharmacy, billing) and usually contains an EMR, which is the clinical record.

What is a PHR?

A personal health record: the patient's own collection of reports, prescriptions and readings, which the patient controls.
