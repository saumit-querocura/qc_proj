How to help elderly parents take their medicines on time

Missed and doubled doses are common when parents take several medicines a day, and harder to spot when you live in another city. A clear list, a simple routine and reminders that tell you when a dose has been taken make the biggest difference.

1. Make one up-to-date list

Gather every prescription and every strip in the house. Write one list with each medicine, its dose, when to take it and what it is for, and ask their doctor or pharmacist to check it, especially if several doctors prescribe. Our free medicine schedule maker prints one.

2. Tie doses to daily habits

Morning tea, lunch and the evening news are easier to remember than clock times. Keep the medicines where that habit happens, away from heat and children.

3. Use a weekly pill organiser

A box with morning, afternoon and night compartments for each day shows at a glance whether a dose was taken. Fill it together once a week, or ask a neighbour or pharmacist to help.

4. Set reminders that close the loop

A reminder only helps if someone knows the dose was taken. With QueroCura, your parent taps Taken on the reminder and, with their permission, you are told straight away. If no confirmation comes, you know to call.

5. Watch for side effects and changes

Dizziness, falls, confusion or new sleepiness after a medicine change are worth a call to the doctor. Never stop or change a medicine without the doctor's advice.

6. Take the list to every visit

Bring the list and the medicines themselves to each appointment so the doctor can review everything together, and update the list the same day.
