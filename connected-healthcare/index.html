Connected healthcare

Connected healthcare: what it is and how it works

Connected healthcare means the people and systems involved in someone's care can share the right information at the right time, with that person's consent: the patient and their family, their doctors, the hospital's departments, labs and pharmacies. Instead of starting from zero at every visit, care picks up where it left off.

How connected healthcare works in India

The Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission provides the public rails: ABHA numbers for people, the Health Facility Registry and the Healthcare Professionals Registry, and a consent framework for moving records between them. On top of those rails sit the systems people actually use: hospital management systems, lab and imaging systems, and personal health record apps.

Standards make the pieces fit: DICOM for medical images, HL7 FHIR for clinical records in the ABDM ecosystem, and consent artefacts that say who may see what, for how long.

Consent is the heart of it

  • Specific: the patient chooses which records, such as medicines and lab results but not symptom history.
  • Time-limited: access ends on a date, or after the episode of care.
  • Revocable: the patient can switch it off at any time.
  • Visible: every view is logged in a record the patient can read.

Benefits

  • Faster starts to treatment, especially in emergencies, because history is available.
  • Fewer repeated tests when earlier results can be seen.
  • Safer prescribing with allergies and current medicines known.
  • Better continuity between specialists, hospitals and home.
  • Less paperwork for patients and staff.

Challenges

  • Uneven adoption: not every hospital or lab is connected yet.
  • Data quality: scanned, handwritten and inconsistent records need checking.
  • Privacy and security: more connections need stronger controls and audit.
  • Trust: patients share only when they understand and control what happens.

Frequently asked questions

What is connected healthcare?

A model of care where patients, families, doctors and hospital departments share the right health information at the right time, with the patient's consent.

What is an example of connected healthcare?

A patient shares their medicines and recent lab results with a hospital before an appointment; the doctor sees them in the visit, adds the new prescription, and it appears back in the patient's own record.

How does ABDM support connected healthcare?

ABDM provides ABHA numbers, registries of facilities and professionals, and a consent framework so records can move between providers when the patient agrees.
