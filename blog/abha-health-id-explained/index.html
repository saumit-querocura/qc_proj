What Is ABHA and Why Should Your Family Have One? A Digital Health ID Explained

The QueroCura Team · September 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Ask most people where their medical records are and you'll get some version of the same answer: scattered. A prescription on a hospital's server, a lab report in a WhatsApp chat, an X-ray on a CD somewhere in a drawer. India's Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission was built to fix exactly this problem — and ABHA is where it starts.

What ABHA actually is

ABHA (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) is a 14-digit digital health ID, similar in spirit to how Aadhaar works for identity — except ABHA is specifically for your health records. It's issued free of cost through India's Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM), and once created, it acts as a key that can link your medical records across participating hospitals, labs, and clinics.

The idea is simple: instead of your history living in a dozen disconnected systems, ABHA gives every participating provider a consistent way to find and, with your consent, access the parts of your medical history relevant to your current care.

Why it actually matters

  • Continuity of care: a new doctor can see relevant history from a previous hospital, instead of starting from zero or relying on your memory of past diagnoses and medications.
  • Fewer repeated tests: if a report already exists and is linked, it doesn't need to be redone just because you switched providers.
  • Faster emergency response: in a crisis, especially if you can't speak for yourself, linked records can help responders make faster, better-informed decisions.
  • You stay in control: access is consent-based — a provider generally needs your explicit approval to view linked records for a specific visit.
The goal isn't a government database that knows everything about you — it's a system where the right doctor can see the right information, only when you say yes.

Where CuraVault fits alongside it

ABHA is infrastructure — a health-ID layer meant to connect participating institutions. But it doesn't replace the need for a personal, always-with-you record of your own health story: your vitals over time, your own symptom history, the medications you're actually taking day to day, the allergies you never want a doctor to miss.

That's what CuraVault is built for inside QueroCura — an encrypted, personal health vault that you control directly, designed to work alongside the broader digital health ecosystem rather than instead of it. It's also what powers QueroCura's Emergency Glass Door: the record that's instantly accessible the moment it matters most, because you built it yourself, ahead of time.

Getting started

Creating an ABHA ID takes a few minutes through the official ABDM app or website, using your Aadhaar or mobile number for verification. It costs nothing. Once you have one, ask your hospital or clinic whether they support linking records to it — adoption is growing but still uneven across providers, which is exactly why keeping your own copy of your health story, through something like CuraVault, remains just as important.

Frequently asked questions

Is ABHA mandatory in India?

No, creating an ABHA (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) is currently voluntary. It's a personal choice, but an increasing number of hospitals and clinics under India's Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission now support linking records to it.

Is my data safe with ABHA?

ABHA is designed with consent-based access — a hospital or doctor can only see your linked records if you explicitly approve access for that specific interaction. That said, always read what you're consenting to before approving any access request.

Do I need ABHA if I already use CuraVault?

They serve complementary purposes. ABHA is the government's health-ID layer for linking records across the wider healthcare system; CuraVault is your own personal, encrypted vault for the records, vitals, and symptom history you want instantly available — including to first responders in an emergency, through QueroCura's own systems.
