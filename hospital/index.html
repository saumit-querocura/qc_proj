How it works

From application to first patient in four steps

1

2

Our team checks every organisation before it can reach any patient. You'll get an e-mail the moment you're approved.

One place for the whole hospital

From the front desk to discharge

🎟️ OPD queue and tokens

Check patients in to a department, call them in, see waits as they grow. A waiting-room TV shows the numbers being served, never names.

🚑 Emergency triage

Five triage levels with time-to-be-seen targets. The sickest are always first, and anyone past their target is flagged.

🛏️ Live bed board

Every ward and bed at a glance: occupied, going home, being cleaned, reserved. Admitting suggests the right free bed.

🧪 Orders between departments

Tests, scans, nursing tasks and consults go to the right team with a priority and a due time. Results flow back to the patient's record.

🤝 Shift handover

Short SBAR handovers per ward or patient, acknowledged by the next shift so nothing falls between them.

🩻 AI-assisted imaging

Upload X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, PET and mammography images. Two independent AI reads draft the findings, with boxes on the image, a differential and its limits. Your clinicians accept or reject each finding and sign the report.

📊 Command centre

Occupancy, waits, overdue orders and department load, live, with the few things that need attention right now.

Questions

Good things to ask

Can we see a patient's data without asking them?

No. The only exception is emergency access, which exists only for patients who turned it on, shows a short essentials view for two hours, needs a written reason from a doctor, and tells the patient immediately.

What does it cost?

We're onboarding our first hospitals now. Get in touch from the contact section on our home page and we'll talk.

What can patients share with us?

They pick from: basics and allergies, vitals, medicines, lab results, scan reports and recent symptom checks. Only results the patient confirmed are shown.

Do you replace our hospital system?

For many clinics and nursing homes it can be the day-to-day system: departments, an OPD token queue with a waiting-room screen, emergency triage, a live bed board, orders between departments, shift handover and discharge checklists. Larger hospitals can run it next to their existing system for the patient-consented parts.

Who can see what at our hospital?

Administrators manage the team, departments and wards. Doctors read and write, order tests, admit and discharge. Nurses read, add vitals and notes, and work nursing tasks and handovers. Front-desk staff connect and check in patients and run the queue and beds, but see no clinical data. Lab and radiology staff see only the test orders sent to them. Operations managers see the command centre and beds, not charts.

Does the AI diagnose scans?

No. It prepares a draft for a qualified clinician: a systematic review of the images, possible findings with where they are, a differential and the limits of what it could see, checked by a second independent pass. It is not a cleared medical device and can be wrong or miss things. Every finding must be accepted or rejected and the report signed by your clinician; patients never see an unsigned draft. Identifying details are removed before images are sent, AI is off until your administrator switches it on, and patients can opt out.

What does the waiting-room screen show?

Token numbers and room names only, never a name. Each screen has its own private link that an administrator can switch off at any time.
